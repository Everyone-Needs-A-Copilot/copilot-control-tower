Five plain-language promises

The invariants, translated out of engineer-speak. Each maps to one of the five arcs below. Flat and quiet on purpose: this is not a marketing hero.

1

It sets up your copilots. You don't.

Control Tower brings in Claude Copilot, Codex Copilot, Knowledge Copilot, and CLI Copilot, and keeps them current, so you never open a terminal.

2

When there's nothing to say, it says nothing.

A quiet icon in your menu bar means everything is current. There is no "all good" popup to dismiss.

3

It only ever asks you about your own things.

Your sign-ins, your unsaved work, a new team you can join. Anything to do with your organization's setup goes to your IT team, never to you.

4

It never touches your unsaved work.

If your own work is in the way of an update, the update waits. Nothing of yours is ever overwritten or thrown away.

5

When an update goes wrong, it keeps the version that worked.

You will not see a scary failure. You will see one calm line telling you your working version was kept.

Arc 1. Invitation & install

Bob has heard about this once: an email with a link. Emotional target is zero anxiety and zero decisions. He should feel this is ordinary, safe, and short. Mostly new design work: the app itself doesn't exist on his Mac yet, so every surface here belongs to email, the browser, Finder, or macOS itself.

  • You never have to set up a shared tool's password. If it's shared, it's just there.
  • You never have to ask anyone to join a team you've been added to.
  • You never have to decide anything about your organization's setup. That's your IT team's, not yours.
  • You never have to check whether it's working. A quiet icon already told you.

What Bob does and sees

Nothing, by design: this is the walkthrough naming, out loud, everything Bob has been spared across the whole journey. Reading the list back to back, the shape of the product becomes obvious: it absorbs work so he never has to see it.

What actually happens

This card exists only in this document; it is not a screen the product ever renders. It is a deliberate summary of the actor-competence routing rule: reversible things Bob can't judge are auto-acted on, things he can't action are escalated to IT, and only non-deferrable decisions about his own data ever reach him.
