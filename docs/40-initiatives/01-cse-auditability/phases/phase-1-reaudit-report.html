The Re-Audit: Adversarial Review & Measurability Map

A second, independent pass over the Phase 1 falsification probe — run as the adversarial review the handoff demanded — plus the answer to the owner's question: what can we measure today, what could we measure, and what should we measure to credibly prove the CSE works. Covers all four products: Claude Copilot and Codex Copilot (one instruction layer, two harnesses), Knowledge Copilot, and CLI Copilot.

RUN 2026-07-12 · SIX INDEPENDENT AUDIT AGENTS · READ-ONLY · NOTHING WAS FIXED OR MODIFIED

The verdict, up front

Phase 1 is trustworthy enough to base product decisions on. A review explicitly instructed to overturn it upheld 13 of 18 findings — several came out stronger — and the three that moved, moved in instructive ways: one headline number was inflated by probe bugs (F-11: "95.6% of knowledge never read" corrects to 67–88%, definition-dependent), the biggest open question resolved against the product (F-16: 1.47 years of shell history shows 14 terminal invocations, ever), and the one verdict that told you to stop trying was wrong (F-12: knowledge efficacy is not structurally unmeasurable — roughly 60–70% of it is benchmarkable with what already exists).

The strategic picture: the binding constraint is still that the ecosystem cannot see itself — but the re-audit found the instruments mostly already exist, unwired: hooks written but registered in 1 repo, a health verb that authenticates every integration, an eval harness for exactly one agent, a voice rubric sitting as prose, and 954 tasks of untapped Task Copilot history reaching back 3 months before the transcript corpus begins. The benchmark tool you want is buildable — and most of its first data sources are already on disk.

Section 2

The three corrections that matter

1 · Knowledge is measurable after all (F-12 falls)

The CEILING verdict was the most consequential claim in Phase 1 because it told you to stop trying. It's wrong, for a specific reason: the product dossiers contain private facts — version numbers, internal module names, former product names, internal domains — that base Claude cannot know. A closed-book Q&A bank generated from the dossiers, scored with-knowledge vs. against-an-empty-tree, is a contamination-immune ablation. And the "voice is prose, not a rubric" premise is simply false: the tone-of-voice doc and the cw extension already carry banned-word lists, an em-dash ban, an AI-cliché list, terminology substitution tables, a sentence-rhythm rule, a reading-level target, and gold before/after exemplar pairs. That is a deterministic linter nobody compiled.

2 · The CLI's usage question is answered, and the answer is near-zero (F-16 resolves)

Phase 1's honest hedge was "terminal usage is invisible, so 14 dead services may be wrong." Shell history closes it: 536 days of near-complete history contain 14 real invocations — 11 of them a single-day dev burst for the uspto feature, whose usage was indeed invisible to transcripts (the hedge was right in mechanism, wrong in magnitude). Every bare copilot command in history (197 of them) hit the cd alias, not the binary. And claude-copilot's cc — the binary Control Tower actually contracts — has been typed into a terminal zero times in its 71-day life.

3 · A headline number was inflated by the probe's own bugs (F-11 corrects)

Two independent agents converged on the same conclusion from different directions. The probe never counted Bash-mediated reads (cat/grep/rg against knowledge paths) — and those outnumber Read-tool reads (586 vs 502 calls; 91 files were read only via Bash). Separately, the never-read join compares historically recorded paths against today's filesystem, and a mid-corpus repo reorganization broke 60% of those joins. The honest range is 67–88% depending on denominator — still a cold library, no longer a dead one.

Section 4

The measurability map — need · possible · should

Your question, answered per component. Measurable today = data already on disk, zero new code. Unlockable = modest instrumentation (config or a small script). Worth measuring = the metrics that actually evidence the value claim, split into adoption (is it used?) and efficacy (does it help?). The re-audit's core discovery is how much of column one already exists unread.

Section 5

Principles the benchmark must respect

  • Usage before efficacy. Phase 1's deepest point survives intact: measuring the quality of knowledge nobody reads, or a CLI nobody invokes, is a category error. The see-itself tier (retention + ledgers) is the foundation, and it is nearly free.
  • Adoption ≠ efficacy — never let one impersonate the other. Most numbers available today are adoption numbers. They can prove the ecosystem is alive; only ablations and rubric-scored evals can prove it helps.
  • Pre-register, then measure. Every definition dispute in this program (F-5, F-11's 67-vs-88 spread, the 671-turn ghost) traces to definitions chosen after seeing data. The claims register precedes the dashboard.
  • The single-user confound is real. Every adoption metric currently measures its own author. Deterministic evals (cc eval, the voice linter, private-fact Q&A) are immune; the ablation suite becomes credible to outsiders only when someone who isn't the author runs it.
  • Claim what you can demonstrate. The inversion finding held: the product that promised least (CLI: "testable") is the only one that delivered. The fastest credibility gain isn't a better benchmark — it's deleting claims that have no check behind them.
Section 6

Blueprint — the benchmark tool you asked for

A web-view side application is the right instinct, and it should obey the same invariant as Control Tower: the CLI computes, the app renders. The existing tools/cse-audit/ harness grows into a bench verb emitting versioned JSON; the web view is a thin skin over its output.

Architecture

  • Collectors: transcript miner (both harnesses), tasks.db reader, health --json, usage ledgers, parity checker, freshness scanner, eval runners — each a deterministic script emitting stamped, versioned JSON (the probe's own lesson: unstamped output goes stale silently).
  • Store: one local SQLite metrics DB with run timestamps — history, not snapshots.
  • Web view: three panels. Adoption (live gauges from today's measurables), Efficacy (ablation deltas and eval pass-rates), Trust (claims register: every public claim, its check, its last verdict — the permanent antidote to F-18).
  • Task packs: the ablation suite takes pluggable persona packs — your software-build tasks today, an accountant's close-the-books tasks later. The harness is constant; the pack is the persona.

Phasing (dependencies, not dates)

  • P0 — Preserve & see: transcript retention (urgent), event ledger, CLI usage ledger, hook registration. Everything else consumes this.
  • P1 — Adoption dashboard: render what already exists — tasks.db history, read-coverage, live integrations, parity, velocity. No new science; immediate visibility.
  • P2 — Efficacy benches: private-fact Q&A, voice linter, cc eval expansion, MCP-twin. Each produces a delta a skeptic can re-run.
  • P3 — The layered ablation: four-config task-suite runs, rubric + anchored-judge scoring, then external pilot users to break the single-author confound. This is the artifact you can show another organization.
Why this is now credible rather than aspirational: P0 is mostly configuration, P1's data is already on disk, and P2's hardest prerequisite — a measurable knowledge layer — was the thing F-12 wrongly declared impossible. The re-audit didn't just grade Phase 1; it established that the benchmark's raw material largely exists.
Section 7

Limitations of this re-audit

  • Warp terminal gap. Warp.app is installed and doesn't reliably write ~/.zsh_history. The 14-invocation figure is a near-complete floor, not a ceiling — close this before quoting "13 dead services" as final.
  • F-7 and F-17 were not re-tested (cohort confounding and the 10% deferral threshold stand unverified).
  • The corpus moved during the review (113→108 sessions); every percentage carries a run timestamp for a reason.
  • One layer of review, not two. Six agents audited the probe and products; nobody has audited the six agents. Their headline numbers (439 commands, 67%, 14 invocations) are file-cited and re-runnable, which is the intended remedy.
  • Probe blind spots found but not fixed (per the no-remediation rule): Fable-class models bucket as "other" in every model-tier split; the never-read join has no rename tracking and will silently re-break on the next repo reorg.
  • Everything here is still single-user data. Nothing in this report is evidence about how a second human would fare.

Method. Six independent audit agents, read-only, 2026-07-12: adversarial probe re-run · new-evidence mining (shell history, test reproduction, binary verification) · four per-product measurability audits. Nothing was repaired, per the handoff's no-remediation rule.
