UX WALKTHROUGH · DRAFT FOR ALIGNMENT

The best connect experience is the one Bob never has.

Bob is an accountant. He has no job to be done with a credential store; his job is to close the month. This walkthrough renders both answers to the open fork: the North Star, where being on his team is the access and no Connect moment ever exists, and the pragmatic first release, where one well designed approval stands in for it until the store can be team gated.

NEW marks a capability today's product does not have. Every quoted string is drafted in the copy deck's voice and is CLI authored: the app renders the sentence, it never composes it (invariant #1). Screens 01 to 03 are what ships today, and they are the reason for the rest.

Order of operations
A DEFECT THAT SURVIVES ANY CREDENTIAL MODEL

This one is sequencing, not copy

The store is reachable and Bob is authorized. His Mac simply does not know yet where the store is, because the step that brings his organization's setup onto the Mac has not run.

Step 6 todayAsks what his team's shared connections are, and reports the answer as a fault.
Step 8 todayBrings his organization's setup, including where the store lives, onto this Mac.

Option B: narrow it

Two honest repairs

Never red, never raw, never alone in a loop

  • Shared rows go quiet rather than red. An outage is not Bob's fault, his fix, or his task.

PART FOUR · THE FLOOR, AND THE FORK

15

Eleven rules that hold whichever way the fork goes

Non-negotiables
UNBLOCKED BY THE DECISION

The floor underneath both answers

Whichever credential model wins, Bob's Mac will one day be offline, or the store will be down, or he will have been removed from a team. On that day the product must say a true sentence that names a human. Today it says raw developer output, or nothing.

01No raw error text, ever, on any Bob surface. The CLI authors a sentence for a person, or the app says nothing. This applies to the admin's screens too.
02No dotfile, Terminal, or file path advice as a user facing step. If the only real fix is a command, it lives behind the existing "show me the step" sheet, and only for someone the product can prove is the admin.
03No secret value on screen, in any form, and no secret names either. Name the service, never the key.
04No state whose only action is impossible. If a row cannot be acted on by the person reading it, it must not be styled as an action.
05No "check again later" without a named next actor. Either Control Tower says it will keep checking, in its own voice, or a human is named.
06No promise the next card contradicts. Either the affordance exists or the sentence goes. This is the honesty violation Bob can personally verify.
07No button on a shared row. A thing his team set up is not his to operate, and the absence of the button is how he learns that without being told.
08No stage exempt from the completion rule while setup still claims success. A stage that cannot fail cannot be trusted when it passes.
09Two situations with different next actors never share a sentence. Unreachable is not removed. Declined is not ineligible.
10Bob is never asked to judge a security event, and never blamed for one. Access loss is stated as a fact about access, with his own work preserved in the same breath.
11No celebration, no time estimate, no percentage. Silence is the success state.
→Fix the floor first. It cannot be wasted work under either answer, and today's screen tells an accountant to edit a dotfile.

Honesty is not the same as working

Making the absence of a connection completely legible removes every dead end, and it makes nothing new work. Bob who is told clearly that his keys are unavailable still cannot close the month. The floor is necessary and it is not the answer.

The one question the owner has to answer

The fork
DECISION NEEDED · OWNER

Is "your organization's shared store must answer to the same sign-in as your GitHub teams" a product requirement, or a recommended configuration with a supported fallback?

Everything in this walkthrough follows from that single fork, and the fork is not technical. It is a choice between an operational cost the organization pays once, and a permanent surface Bob and his admin pay for every day.
