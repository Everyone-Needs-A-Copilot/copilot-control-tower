    <section id="north-star">
      <p class="section-kicker">The goal-driven target</p>
      <h2>The Copilot Solution Ecosystem</h2>

      <div class="north-star-intro">
        <p><strong>The Copilot Solution Ecosystem should give ordinary people the practical capabilities of a highly technical, multidisciplinary solution team.</strong></p>
        <p>A person should be able to begin with a real problem—not with software configuration—and receive the right combination of company knowledge, departmental expertise, specialist methods, tools, and guided workflows needed to create a useful solution. That solution might be software, automation, research, analysis, documentation, a service, or a physical-digital experience.</p>
        <p>The ecosystem should make the result specific to the person, their department, and their organization—not generic AI output—without requiring them to understand terminals, repositories, agents, manifests, or infrastructure.</p>
        <p>It should also remove Pablo as the person who must manually connect everything. Improvements made once at the appropriate level should safely reach everyone entitled to receive them.</p>
      </div>

      <h3 class="subsection">What each component does</h3>
      <table class="evidence-table">
        <thead>
          <tr><th scope="col">Component</th><th scope="col">Plain-language role</th></tr>
        </thead>
        <tbody>
          <tr><td><strong>Knowledge Copilot</strong></td><td>Supplies the knowledge and expertise: company context, standards, methodologies, department practices, writing guidance, research, and reusable skills.</td></tr>
          <tr><td><strong>Claude Copilot</strong></td><td>Supplies one execution environment: specialist agents, workflows, commands, safety rules, and the protocol that decides which specialists should help with a problem.</td></tr>
          <tr><td><strong>Codex Copilot</strong></td><td>Supplies the equivalent operating model inside Codex, so Codex can use the same specialist roles, standards, and project practices instead of behaving like an unrelated generic coding assistant.</td></tr>
          <tr><td><strong><code>cc</code> CLI</strong></td><td>Owns ecosystem truth. It discovers layers, resolves precedence, installs material into projects, updates it, verifies it, and runs conformance checks. Other surfaces should render its conclusions rather than recomputing them.</td></tr>
          <tr><td><strong>CLI Copilot / <code>copilot</code> CLI</strong></td><td>Gives solutions controlled access to operational services such as infrastructure, deployment, secrets references, integrations, and communications. It provides capabilities without embedding credentials in inherited content.</td></tr>
          <tr><td><strong>Task Copilot / <code>tc</code></strong></td><td>Preserves execution state: plans, tasks, decisions, handoffs, test evidence, and durable work products. It allows complex solution work to continue across agents and sessions without relying on chat history.</td></tr>
          <tr><td><strong>Copilot Control Tower</strong></td><td>Makes the ecosystem installable and dependable for a non-technical person. It supervises <code>cc</code>/<code>copilot</code>, keeps the machine synchronized, and reports verified status in plain language. It does not decide ecosystem state itself.</td></tr>
          <tr><td><strong>The project or solution</strong></td><td>The destination of all this support. It remains self-contained and owned by the people creating it. Control Tower distributes the tools used to build solutions; it does not synchronize or take ownership of the solutions themselves.</td></tr>
        </tbody>
      </table>

      <p>Claude and Codex are two execution faces of the same ecosystem. They should differ where their runtimes require it, but they should receive the same organizational intent, specialist model, safety posture, and knowledge.</p>
      <p>There are also two distinct command-line responsibilities:</p>
      <ul class="plain-list">
        <li><code>cc</code> manages and verifies the Copilot ecosystem.</li>
        <li><code>copilot</code> provides operational and service capabilities.</li>
      </ul>
      <p>They should integrate, but they should not duplicate each other's decisions.</p>

      <h3 class="subsection">How the layers work</h3>
      <p>Each of the four component families—Claude, Codex, CLI, and Knowledge—can participate at four levels. Together, that creates the 16-layer ecosystem.</p>
      <table class="evidence-table">
        <thead>
          <tr><th scope="col">Layer</th><th scope="col">What belongs there</th></tr>
        </thead>
        <tbody>
          <tr><td><strong>Foundation / Core</strong></td><td>Universal capabilities that should work for everyone: specialist roles, general methods, security rules, basic commands, and safe defaults.</td></tr>
          <tr><td><strong>Organization</strong></td><td>What makes the ecosystem belong to this company: its purpose, language, operating principles, shared services, policies, standards, and organization-wide methods.</td></tr>
          <tr><td><strong>Department</strong></td><td>What makes the help appropriate to a particular discipline or team: accounting practices, legal workflows, product methods, technical conventions, or other domain expertise.</td></tr>
          <tr><td><strong>Personal</strong></td><td>What belongs only to one person: their preferences, private knowledge, memory, working style, and intentional personal overrides. It must never leak upward into shared layers.</td></tr>
        </tbody>
      </table>
      <p>The layers should behave like inheritance, not four separate installations:</p>
      <ul class="plain-list">
        <li>Foundation fills the gaps.</li>
        <li>Organization makes the foundation company-specific.</li>
        <li>Department makes it appropriate to the person's area of work.</li>
        <li>Personal makes it useful to the individual.</li>
      </ul>
      <p>When a nearer layer intentionally replaces something, the nearer version should win. When content is additive, the applicable layers should combine safely. If a layer has nothing substantive to contribute, it should fall through to the next layer rather than shadowing useful content with an empty placeholder.</p>

      <h3 class="subsection">How everything should work together</h3>
      <p>The intended journey is:</p>
      <ol class="steps">
        <li><strong>Access establishes entitlement.</strong> GitHub repository access determines which organization, department, and personal layers a person may receive.</li>
        <li><strong><code>cc</code> resolves the correct ecosystem.</strong> It reads the signed manifests and pins, walks the four-layer hierarchy, and determines the exact tools and content applicable to that person and project.</li>
        <li><strong>The resolved result is installed and recorded.</strong> Claude agents, Codex skills, commands, knowledge, hooks, and operational integrations are materialized into the project. The lock records where they came from and what was installed.</li>
        <li><strong>Control Tower keeps it working.</strong> It invokes the CLI on a safe cadence, renders verified status, and asks the person only about decisions that genuinely belong to them.</li>
        <li><strong>The person begins with a problem.</strong> They should not need to choose infrastructure or assemble agents manually.</li>
        <li><strong>The protocol assembles the right specialist workflow.</strong> Depending on the work, this might involve service design, UX, visual design, architecture, engineering, QA, security, documentation, operations, finance, or another specialist combination.</li>
        <li><strong>The specialists receive the right context.</strong> Their general expertise comes from the foundation, their company understanding from the organization, their domain expertise from the department, and their personal context from the individual layer.</li>
        <li><strong>Operational tools help turn the work into reality.</strong> The ecosystem can research, design, build, test, deploy, document, and operate a solution while keeping credentials and authority within the proper boundaries.</li>
        <li><strong>Task Copilot preserves the evidence.</strong> Plans, decisions, implementation records, verification, and handoffs remain durable across sessions.</li>
        <li><strong>The resulting solution belongs to its creators.</strong> It remains a self-contained project. The ecosystem can keep improving the tools used to create it without taking control of the product itself.</li>
      </ol>

      <h3 class="subsection">The outcome this should produce</h3>
      <p>When the system works, people should be able to create solutions that are:</p>
      <ul class="good-list">
        <li>grounded in a real human need;</li>
        <li>informed by company and department knowledge;</li>
        <li>produced through appropriate specialist methods;</li>
        <li>technically and operationally sound;</li>
        <li>secure and auditable;</li>
        <li>specific rather than generic;</li>
        <li>maintainable after the original session ends;</li>
        <li>achievable without Pablo manually wiring the system together.</li>
      </ul>
      <p>The organization should be able to improve its collective ability once—by improving the proper shared layer—and have that improvement safely reach the people and projects that should receive it.</p>
      <p>The individual should gain leverage without surrendering ownership or privacy.</p>

      <h3 class="subsection">Important boundaries</h3>
      <p>This ecosystem is not meant to become:</p>
      <ul class="plain-list">
        <li>a generic chatbot layered over company documents;</li>
        <li>an autonomous decision-maker that replaces human judgment;</li>
        <li>a fleet-management or employee-monitoring system;</li>
        <li>a mechanism for synchronizing the products people build;</li>
        <li>a path through which personal content can move into shared repositories;</li>
        <li>a second implementation of state already computed by <code>cc</code>;</li>
        <li>a system that appears healthy when it cannot prove that it is.</li>
      </ul>
      <p>The simplest expression of the goal is:</p>
      <blockquote class="goal-quote">
        <p>Give every person access to a trustworthy, company-aware, multidisciplinary solution team—one that helps them turn real problems into working solutions without requiring technical expertise, while safely carrying the organization's accumulated knowledge and capabilities to them.</p>
      </blockquote>
      <p><strong>The ultimate goal is democratizing high-quality solution creation, not merely installing and maintaining a collection of copilots.</strong></p>
