## Concepts considered before converging (interaction structure, not visual variation)

1. **Provisioning as a visible act with an invisible payload** — a "Set up your team's keys" step that runs, shows progress, and reports success. Nielsen: strong on #1 visibility of system status; fails #6 recognition (invents a subject Bob has no model for), fails #8 minimalism (a step whose only content is that it succeeded), and fails #7 flexibility for the returning user (a step he must dismiss on every new Mac forever). **Rejected:** it manufactures the mental model the whole design exists to delete.
2. **Provisioning as a receipt only** (selected) — no act, no step, no announcement; step 6 reports state and screens 03/05 report change. Nielsen: strongest on #2 match to the real world (his teams are the model he already has), #5 error prevention (no state whose only action is impossible can be rendered), #8 minimalism. Weakest on #1 visibility, which is answered deliberately: visibility is owed for things the user can act on, and every failure state here still names an actor.
3. **Provisioning as an ambient status surface** — a persistent menu-bar section showing entitlement, scope and freshness. Nielsen: strong on #1 and #10 documentation; fails #8 badly, and fails #6 by making a backstage fact permanently frontstage. **Rejected as a Bob surface, adopted for the administrator** (screen 09's rotation clock is exactly this pattern, in the place where the reader can act on it).

The selection rationale is recorded on the screens themselves rather than as a separate concept section, because the series renders decisions as consequences.

## State coverage

The eight states are covered across the set rather than per component, as the series does. Default (02, 06, 07), hover/active (not applicable in a static UX walkthrough; the UIDS companion owns them, noted here so the omission is deliberate), focus (deferred to the UIDS stage with the series' precedent), disabled (01's Continue while the device flow is pending; 10's steps 5-7 blocked-by), loading (01 waiting-in-browser, 09's rotation progress), error (04's four-card degrade grid, 05's removal, 06's refusal variant, 07's refused wildcard), empty (05's today card, where the empty shared-connections state is a sentence with an actor rather than an absence).

## Microinteraction definitions carried by the screens

- **Entitlement arrives (screen 03).** Trigger: a system event nobody initiated, the mapping pull. Rules: the row simply exists; no badge, no banner, no modal. Feedback: one past-tense line under What changed. Loops: none by design; the event is not repeated, escalated, or reminded. The deliberate claim is that the strongest interaction here has no trigger the user can perceive.
- **Check unavailable (screen 04).** Trigger: a failed check on the read path. Rules: fail closed, never reuse past TTL, distinguish unreachable from removed. Feedback: sentence 1 with Control Tower as actor; after escalation, sentence 2 with a named human plus Copy details for support (value-free). Loops: the escalation is the only mode change, and it happens once.
- **Prove before claiming (screen 06).** Trigger: the administrator finishes the store step. Rules: run both the positive and the negative read. Feedback: two check rows, one of which is a check mark on a refused read. Loops: on failure the claim is withheld rather than retried silently.
- **Widening review (screen 07).** Trigger: a proposed mapping change. Rules: constraints refuse wildcards and unknown teams before review; a widening requires a second administrator. Feedback: the change rendered as an English sentence, not a diff. Loops: nothing takes effect until the review lands.

## Accessibility

Keyboard: the document is a linear reading order, tab order follows DOM order (header title, TOC 01-13, theme toggle, then per section the window's interactive mocks and the pager links). Skip-target behaviour is provided by the sticky TOC with `scroll-margin-top` on every section. Screen reader: `nav aria-label="Screens"`, `button aria-label` on the theme cycle, `aria-hidden` on the decorative aviator glyph, empty decorative `<i>` for window dots. Colour is never the only channel anywhere: every status carries glyph + word + position (e.g. Ready/Pass/Live/Blocked by 5/Not needed alongside ok/warn/danger colour). Contrast tokens are inherited unchanged from 05/15, which are the AA-checked set. `prefers-reduced-motion` disables smooth scrolling; `color-scheme: light dark` plus a three-state auto/light/dark toggle.

## Voice compliance

Quiet Instrument throughout. No em-dashes anywhere except the series-standard `<title>` (verified by grep). No celebration, no percentages as promises, no time estimates on Bob surfaces. The words OIDC, JWT, broker, token, assertion, Infisical, credential and keychain appear on NO Bob surface: his screens say "your team's shared connections", "shared keys", "the check". Admin surfaces name components precisely (machine identity, issuer, discovery address, `Members: read`, `team_scopes`, scope claim, audience) but use "the membership service" as the product-facing name for the broker, with the design's own vocabulary reserved for the margin notes so a reviewer can map back to the source. No secret value, masked value, or prefix appears anywhere; no secret NAMES either, which is why screen 10 step 7 is described by its effect rather than by naming the two variables.
